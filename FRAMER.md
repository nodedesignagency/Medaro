  - **Safari clips fit text to its line box** (user's iPhone, 2026-11-21: "'for' is cutting"). Framer renders fit text as an SVG `foreignObject`, and WebKit ignores `overflow: visible` there, so with the old 0.7em box the ascenders of d, f, l, h (0.720em) were cut on iPhone and Mac Safari while Chrome showed them. **Rule: never give fit text a line height below 1.1em, and re-run autofit after changing the line height** (Framer computes the fit box once, at autofit; changing the line height afterwards moves the letters inside the old box).
  - **Trim, as `text-box: trim-both cap alphabetic`:** each line sits in two frames whose aspect ratios make their heights em-proportional (fit text scales with width, so ratios scale with it): the outer frame is cap-top to baseline (`width / (0.7 × font size)`: Made for 5.594, Better Health 8.301; stack, bottom-aligned), the inner frame is box-top to baseline (`width / (0.796 × font size)`: 4.920, 7.300; top-aligned) and holds the 1.1em text, which overflows it at the bottom. Both frames need `minHeight: 0` (otherwise CSS grows them to the text and the ratio is ignored). The outer frame's top is the cap top and its bottom is the baseline, at any width: Head `top 0`, sentence 40px under the baseline, Better Health baseline 32px above the cards (wrapper `paddingBottom 32`). Measured against `main` at 1440: cap top 115 vs 114, baselines 206 / 633 vs 206 / 632, right edge 1372 = 1372; same at 1280 and 1920. Checked with Safari's clip forced on in the canvas: no letter is cut.

- Products: one box (`aspectRatio 0.62`, pinned top/bottom from the top of "Made for" to the top of "Better Health.", centred).
